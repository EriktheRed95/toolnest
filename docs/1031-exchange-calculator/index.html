1031 Exchange Calculator

Estimate the taxes deferred by a 1031 like-kind exchange of investment property. See your realized gain, capital gains and depreciation recapture tax, and net proceeds to reinvest.

How a 1031 exchange defers tax

When you sell an appreciated investment property outright, you owe tax on the realized gain — the sale price minus selling costs and your adjusted cost basis. That gain is taxed in two layers: depreciation you previously deducted is "recaptured" (often at up to 25%), and the remaining appreciation is taxed at capital gains rates, plus any state income tax. A Section 1031 like-kind exchange lets you roll the entire proceeds into a replacement investment property and postpone all of that tax, carrying your old basis forward.

This calculator estimates the tax you would defer by splitting your gain into a depreciation-recapture portion and a capital-gains portion, applying your rates, and adding state tax. It also shows the net proceeds available to reinvest. Remember the deferral is conditional: you must use a qualified intermediary, reinvest all proceeds into equal-or-greater value, replace any paid-off debt, and hit the strict 45-day and 180-day deadlines.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 1031 exchange?
A Section 1031 like-kind exchange lets you sell an investment or business property and reinvest the proceeds into another qualifying property while deferring the capital gains and depreciation recapture taxes you would otherwise owe. The tax is postponed, not erased — it carries over into the replacement property's basis.
What taxes does a 1031 exchange defer?
It defers federal capital gains tax on appreciation, depreciation recapture tax on the depreciation you previously claimed (often taxed at up to 25%), and in most cases applicable state income tax on the gain. This calculator estimates each piece and totals the tax you'd defer.
What are the 1031 deadlines?
Two strict clocks start when you sell: you must identify replacement property within 45 days, and complete the purchase within 180 days. Both are calendar-day deadlines with essentially no extensions, and a qualified intermediary must hold the proceeds in between.
Do I have to reinvest everything?
To fully defer tax, you generally must reinvest all net proceeds and acquire property of equal or greater value, replacing any debt that was paid off. Cash you keep (called 'boot') or a drop in debt can trigger partial taxable gain. This estimate assumes a full deferral.
