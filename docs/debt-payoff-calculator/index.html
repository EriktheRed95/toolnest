Snowball or avalanche — get out of debt faster

When you're juggling several debts, the order you attack them in matters more than most people realize. Two strategies dominate, and they optimize for different things:

  • Debt avalanche — pay minimums on everything, then throw every extra dollar at the highest-interest debt first. This minimizes total interest and is almost always the fastest, cheapest path mathematically.
  • Debt snowball — pay minimums on everything, then attack the smallest balance first. You clear individual debts quickly, and those early wins build momentum that helps many people stick with the plan.

This calculator simulates both on your actual debts and shows the difference in months and interest, so you can weigh the avalanche's savings against the snowball's motivational payoff.

The real engine: extra payments

Minimum payments are structured to keep you in debt for years, especially on high-rate credit cards. The single biggest factor in how fast you get free isn't which method you pick — it's how much extra you can put toward the target debt each month. As each debt is cleared, roll its payment into the next one; that growing payment is what gives the snowball (and avalanche) their power.

Frequently asked questions

What is the debt snowball method?
The snowball method pays minimums on every debt and throws all extra money at the smallest balance first. When it's gone, you roll its payment into the next-smallest, and so on. It's slower mathematically but delivers quick early wins that keep many people motivated to stay the course.
What is the debt avalanche method?
The avalanche method pays minimums on everything and directs all extra money to the debt with the highest interest rate first, then the next highest. It minimizes total interest and usually pays everything off fastest, though the first win can take longer if your highest-rate debt has a large balance.
Which is better, snowball or avalanche?
Avalanche saves the most money and time on paper. Snowball can save your motivation, which matters because the best plan is the one you actually finish. This calculator shows both so you can see the dollar difference and decide whether the avalanche's savings outweigh the snowball's psychological boost.
How much faster will extra payments clear my debt?
A lot. Minimum payments are designed to keep you in debt for years; even a modest extra amount each month, focused on one debt at a time, can cut the payoff timeline dramatically and save thousands in interest. The bigger the extra payment, the larger the effect.
