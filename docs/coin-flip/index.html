A coin has no memory

If you've just seen five heads in a row, it's tempting to feel that tails is "due." It isn't. Each flip is independent of every flip before it, so the odds reset to 50/50 every single time. Believing past results change future ones is the classic gambler's fallacy — and it's exactly why a coin flip is such an honest tiebreaker: nothing you or the coin did earlier can tilt the next outcome.

Use it to choose who goes first, settle a friendly debate, break a deadlock between two equally good choices, or simply add a little chance to your day. It's the simplest unbiased decision-maker there is.

Frequently asked questions

Is this coin flip truly random?
Yes. Each flip uses your browser's built-in random number generator to pick heads or tails with an equal 50/50 chance, independent of previous flips. There's no bias and no pattern — just like a fair physical coin.
Can I flip more than one coin?
Yes. Set the number of coins and flip them all at once. The tool shows each result and a running tally of how many heads and tails you've gotten across all your flips this session.
Does a previous result affect the next flip?
No. Each flip is independent — a coin has no memory. Even after five heads in a row, the next flip is still a 50/50 chance. Expecting tails to be 'due' is the gambler's fallacy.
What can I use a coin flip for?
Settling a decision between two options, breaking a tie, choosing who goes first in a game, or any time you want an unbiased yes/no or this/that answer. It's the simplest fair way to let chance decide.
