Home Appreciation Calculator

Project a home's future value from an annual appreciation rate, and see total gain, percentage growth, and value at key milestones.

Projecting what your home could be worth

Home appreciation is the slow, compounding force behind much of household wealth. This calculator takes today's value and an annual appreciation rate and projects forward, compounding each year so the growth accelerates over time. It reports the projected future value, the total dollar gain, the percentage growth, and the average gain per year — plus a milestone table showing where the value lands at 5, 10, 15, 20, and 30 years when your horizon reaches them.

Compounding is the key idea: a 3% gain on a $400,000 home is $12,000 in year one, but the same 3% applies to a larger base each year, so later years add more dollars. That's why even modest appreciation rates produce striking totals over a couple of decades. Remember that the rate you enter is nominal — to gauge real purchasing power, compare it against expected inflation.

Estimate only. Appreciation is not guaranteed — home values vary by market and can fall as well as rise. This projection assumes a single, steady annual rate and ignores transaction costs, taxes, and maintenance. This is not financial or investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

How is home appreciation calculated?
This tool compounds the value annually: future value equals current value times (1 + annual rate) raised to the number of years. So a $400,000 home appreciating 3% a year is worth about $537,566 after 10 years — compounding makes later years add more dollars than earlier ones.
What is a realistic home appreciation rate?
Long-run U.S. home appreciation has historically averaged somewhere around 3% to 5% per year nominally, but it varies enormously by location and era — some markets boom, others stagnate or fall. A conservative default of 3% is a reasonable starting assumption, but your local market is what matters.
Does appreciation account for inflation?
Not by default. The rate you enter is nominal — it includes general inflation. If overall prices rise 3% a year and your home appreciates 3%, its real (inflation-adjusted) value is roughly flat. To estimate real growth, enter a rate above your expected inflation.
Is home appreciation guaranteed?
No. Home values can and do fall, sometimes sharply, as many owners learned in 2008. Appreciation depends on local supply and demand, interest rates, the economy, and the condition of your home. Treat any projection as a scenario, not a promise.
