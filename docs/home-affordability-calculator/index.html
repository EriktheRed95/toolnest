Home Affordability Calculator

Find out how much house you can afford based on your income, debts, down payment, and interest rate. See your maximum home price and target monthly payment.

How much house can you really afford?

Affordability isn't about the biggest loan a bank will hand you — it's about the payment you can comfortably carry. Lenders decide using your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio: the share of your gross (pre-tax) monthly income that goes to debt payments. This calculator starts from a DTI limit you choose, subtracts your existing monthly debts, and solves for the home price whose full payment fits in what's left.

The full payment isn't just principal and interest. It includes property taxes and insurance (which lenders collect in escrow) and any HOA dues — and because taxes scale with the home's value, a more expensive house costs more on every line. That's why a modest change in rate or down payment can move your maximum price by tens of thousands of dollars.

The 28/36 rule of thumb

A time-tested guideline says keep housing at or below 28% of gross monthly income (the front-end ratio) and all debts at or below 36% (the back-end ratio). Many programs stretch the back-end ratio to 43% or higher, which raises the price you can technically qualify for — but borrowing to the limit leaves little room for emergencies, maintenance, or rate changes on adjustable loans.

Estimate only. This is a planning tool, not a pre-approval or financial advice. Your real buying power depends on your credit, assets, employment, loan program, and a lender's underwriting. Get pre-approved before you shop.

Frequently asked questions

How much house can I afford on my salary?
Lenders look at your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio rather than salary alone. A common guideline is that your total monthly debts — including the new mortgage — should stay at or below 36%–43% of your gross monthly income. This calculator works backward from that limit, your down payment, and current rates to estimate the maximum home price you'd qualify for.
What is the 28/36 rule?
It's a classic affordability guideline: spend no more than 28% of gross monthly income on housing (the front-end ratio) and no more than 36% on all debt payments combined (the back-end ratio). Many loan programs allow higher back-end ratios — often up to 43% or more — but staying near 28/36 keeps payments comfortable.
Does my down payment change how much I can afford?
Yes. A larger down payment means a smaller loan for the same home price, so your monthly payment is lower and you can afford a higher-priced home within the same DTI limit. It can also help you avoid PMI once you reach 20% down.
Is this a pre-approval?
No. This is a planning estimate. A lender's pre-approval also considers your credit score, employment history, assets, and the specific loan program, and uses your verified income and debts. Use this to set a target price range, then get pre-approved before shopping seriously.
