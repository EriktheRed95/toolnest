Cap Rate, GRM & 1% Rule Calculator

Screen a rental property fast with three classic metrics: capitalization rate, gross rent multiplier, and the 1% rule.

Three quick screens for a rental

Investors look at dozens of properties for every one they buy, so they need fast filters. This calculator runs three of the classics at once. The cap rate divides net operating income by price to estimate an all-cash yield, letting you compare properties regardless of how they're financed. The gross rent multiplier divides price by annual rent — a lower number means you pay less per dollar of rent. The 1% rule checks whether monthly rent reaches 1% of the purchase price, a rough threshold for whether a deal can cash flow.

None of these is a verdict. They're tripwires that tell you which listings deserve a real underwrite and which to skip. A property can pass the 1% rule and still lose money after a big roof repair, or fail it and shine because of appreciation potential.

  • Cap rate — NOI ÷ price. Ignores your loan; describes the property.
  • GRM — price ÷ gross rent. Ignores expenses; a blunt valuation check.
  • 1% rule — monthly rent ÷ price. A go/no-go shortlist filter.

Estimate only. These are screening metrics, not a full analysis. They omit financing, vacancy, capital expenditures, and market trends. Verify rents and expenses and underwrite carefully before buying. This is not financial or investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cap rate?
The capitalization rate is a property's net operating income (NOI) divided by its purchase price, expressed as a percentage. It estimates the unleveraged annual yield — what the property would return if you paid all cash. Higher cap rates suggest more income relative to price, often paired with more risk or a weaker location.
What is the gross rent multiplier (GRM)?
GRM is the purchase price divided by the gross annual rent. It's a rough valuation shortcut: a lower GRM means you're paying less for each dollar of rent. Because it ignores expenses, it's only useful as a first-pass screen for comparing similar properties.
What is the 1% rule?
The 1% rule says a rental's monthly rent should be at least 1% of the purchase price — so a $200,000 home should rent for about $2,000/month. It's a quick filter to decide whether a deal is worth a closer look, not a guarantee of cash flow.
Are these metrics enough to evaluate a deal?
No. Cap rate, GRM, and the 1% rule are screening tools that help you reject obvious misfits quickly. A full analysis needs financing terms, vacancy, capital expenditures, management, and local market trends. Use these to shortlist, then underwrite the survivors carefully.
