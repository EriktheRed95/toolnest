CD Calculator

Calculate the maturity value and total interest of a certificate of deposit from your deposit, APY, and term length.

What a CD earns

A certificate of deposit (CD) is a savings product where you agree to leave a lump sum with a bank or credit union for a fixed term — a few months to several years — in exchange for a guaranteed interest rate that's usually higher than a regular savings account. This calculator takes your deposit, the advertised APY, and the term, and shows what the CD will be worth at maturity along with the total interest earned.

Because the APY a bank quotes already accounts for compounding, the math is clean: the deposit grows by the APY once per year for the length of the term. The tradeoff for that locked-in, predictable return is access — pull your money out before the term ends and you'll typically owe an early-withdrawal penalty. CDs at insured institutions are very low risk for your principal, which makes them popular for money you want to grow safely on a known timeline.

  • Maturity value — deposit grown by the APY over the full term.
  • Total interest — what you earn above your original deposit.
  • APY — the true annual yield, with compounding included.

Estimate only. APY accounts for compounding, but actual returns depend on your bank's terms; early withdrawal usually incurs a penalty that can reduce or erase interest. Verify rates and terms before depositing. This is not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How is CD interest calculated?
Because APY already reflects how often interest compounds, this tool grows your deposit by the APY once per year: maturity value equals deposit times (1 + APY) raised to the number of years. A $10,000 CD at 4.5% APY for 1 year matures at $10,450; the same CD for 2 years matures at about $10,920.
What's the difference between APY and interest rate?
The interest rate is the nominal rate before compounding. APY (annual percentage yield) folds compounding in, so it shows the true yearly return. Banks advertise CDs by APY, which is why this calculator uses it directly — no need to specify a compounding frequency separately.
What happens if I withdraw from a CD early?
Most CDs charge an early-withdrawal penalty, often several months of interest, if you take your money out before the term ends. That penalty can erase much of your earnings, so only commit funds you won't need until maturity. Some 'no-penalty' CDs exist but usually pay a lower APY.
Are CDs a safe investment?
CDs at FDIC-insured banks (or NCUA-insured credit unions) are insured up to the legal limit, making them very low risk for the principal. The main risks are inflation outpacing your APY and the opportunity cost of locking up money you might otherwise invest for higher returns.
