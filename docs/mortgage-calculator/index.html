Understanding your mortgage payment

Your monthly housing cost is usually more than just the loan. Lenders bundle it into PITI — Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance — and collect the tax and insurance portions in an escrow account so the big annual bills are spread across 12 months. If your down payment is under 20%, most conventional loans add private mortgage insurance (PMI), and homes in a community association add HOA dues. This calculator adds all of these so the number you see is closer to what you'll actually pay.

The principal-and-interest portion is fixed for the life of a fixed-rate loan, but in the early years most of each payment goes to interest. The amortization schedule above shows how the balance falls and how the principal/interest split shifts over time — which is why making extra principal payments early saves the most interest.

Tip for buyers: taxes and insurance vary a lot by area. Property tax is often quoted as a percentage of value (roughly 0.5%–2.5%/yr depending on the state and county), and insurance depends on the home and location. Plug in local figures for a realistic estimate, then confirm everything with a lender's official Loan Estimate before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in a monthly mortgage payment?
A typical payment has four parts, often called PITI: Principal and Interest (the loan itself), plus property Taxes and homeowner's Insurance held in escrow. Many payments also include private mortgage insurance (PMI) if your down payment is under 20%, and HOA dues if applicable. This calculator adds all of them.
How is the principal and interest payment calculated?
It uses the standard amortization formula: M = P × r(1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the number of monthly payments. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal.
When does PMI go away?
On most conventional loans, PMI can be removed once you reach about 20% equity (an 80% loan-to-value ratio), and lenders must automatically cancel it at 22% equity based on the original schedule. This estimate includes PMI in the monthly figure while your down payment is under 20%.
Is this calculator's result exact?
It's a close estimate for planning. Your actual payment depends on your exact rate, taxes, insurance premiums, loan program, and lender fees. Always confirm with a lender's official Loan Estimate.
