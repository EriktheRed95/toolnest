Credit Card Payoff Calculator

Find out how long it will take to pay off your credit card and how much interest you'll pay. Enter your balance, APR, and monthly payment to see your payoff timeline.

How long until your card is paid off?

Credit card debt is some of the most expensive debt most people carry, with APRs often above 20%. This calculator simulates your payoff month by month: it adds interest to your balance, subtracts your fixed monthly payment, and counts how many months it takes to reach zero — while tallying the total interest you'll pay. It also warns you if your payment is too small to cover the monthly interest, in which case the balance never falls.

The big lesson is almost always the same: pay more than the minimum. Minimums are designed to keep the balance lingering for years, with most of your money going to interest rather than principal. A fixed, higher payment flips that ratio and gets you out of debt far faster.

  • Pay a fixed amount above the minimum every month, not just whatever's "left over."
  • Stop adding charges while you pay it down so you're not refilling the balance.
  • Consider a balance transfer to a lower or 0% intro rate if you qualify — more of each payment then hits the principal.

Estimate only. This assumes a fixed balance, rate, and monthly payment with no new charges. Real cards have changing minimums, variable rates, and fees, so your actual payoff may differ. This is not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How is credit card interest calculated?
Most cards charge interest monthly based on your balance and APR. Each month roughly balance × (APR / 12) is added as interest. This calculator applies that interest, subtracts your payment, and repeats until the balance reaches zero — counting the months and totaling the interest you pay along the way.
What happens if my payment doesn't cover the interest?
If your monthly payment is less than the interest charged that month, your balance grows instead of shrinking and the card never gets paid off. The calculator warns you when this happens. You must pay more than the monthly interest for the balance to fall at all — and ideally far more to make real progress.
Why is paying only the minimum so expensive?
Minimum payments are often set near the interest charge, so most of your money goes to interest and the balance barely moves. That can stretch repayment over a decade or more and cost far more in interest than the original purchases. Paying a fixed, larger amount each month dramatically shortens the timeline.
How can I pay off my card faster?
Pay a fixed amount well above the minimum every month, avoid adding new charges, and consider a lower-rate balance transfer or personal loan if you qualify. Even a modest increase in your monthly payment can cut months or years off the payoff and save hundreds or thousands in interest.
