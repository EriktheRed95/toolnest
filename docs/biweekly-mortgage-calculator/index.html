Biweekly Mortgage Calculator

See how switching to biweekly mortgage payments pays off your loan faster. Compare biweekly vs monthly payments and the time and interest you'd save.

Why biweekly payments pay your mortgage off faster

The trick behind biweekly mortgage payments is hiding in the calendar. Instead of 12 monthly payments a year, you pay half your monthly amount every two weeks. There are 52 weeks in a year, so that's 26 half-payments — the equivalent of 13 full monthly payments. That one extra payment, applied entirely to principal, is what shaves years off your loan and saves you interest.

On a typical 30-year mortgage, switching to a biweekly schedule commonly cuts 4–6 years off the term. This calculator shows your biweekly payment, the time saved, and exactly how much interest you'd avoid.

You don't need a special program

Some lenders sell "biweekly payment programs," occasionally with setup or per-payment fees. You usually don't need them. The entire benefit comes from making one extra payment's worth of principal each year, which you can do for free by:

  • Adding 1/12 of your payment to each monthly payment, or
  • Making one extra full payment once a year, or
  • Setting up biweekly drafts yourself, if your servicer applies them correctly.

However you do it, confirm the extra is applied to principal — that's where the savings live. And don't pay a fee for something you can replicate at no cost.

Estimate only. Figures use principal and interest; taxes and insurance are separate. Some servicers hold biweekly payments and apply them monthly, which changes the result. Confirm how yours handles them. This is not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How do biweekly mortgage payments work?
Instead of one monthly payment, you pay half that amount every two weeks. Because there are 52 weeks in a year, you make 26 half-payments — equal to 13 full monthly payments instead of 12. That one extra payment per year goes entirely to principal and shortens your loan, typically by several years.
How much does biweekly payment save?
On a typical 30-year loan, paying biweekly often cuts about 4–6 years off the term and saves a substantial amount of interest, depending on your rate and balance. The savings come purely from the equivalent of one extra monthly payment each year applied to principal.
Do I need to enroll with my lender?
Not necessarily. Some lenders offer official biweekly programs (sometimes with a fee), but you can usually achieve the same result for free by paying 1/12 of a payment extra each month, or by making one extra full payment per year. The math is identical — just make sure extra goes to principal.
Is biweekly payment worth it?
If the extra annual payment fits your budget, it's an easy, automatic way to pay off your mortgage faster and save interest. But there's nothing magic about the biweekly schedule itself — the savings come from paying more principal. If you'd rather keep flexibility, making occasional extra principal payments achieves the same thing.
