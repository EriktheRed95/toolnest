Understanding loan-to-value

Loan-to-value compares how much you owe to what the property is worth. It's one of the first numbers a lender looks at, because it measures their risk: a high LTV means you have little equity cushion, so a default is more likely to leave the lender short. That's why a lower LTV usually unlocks better interest rates, more loan options, and — on conventional loans — freedom from private mortgage insurance.

The 80% threshold is the one to remember. At or below 80% LTV (at least 20% equity), conventional borrowers generally skip PMI. Between 80% and roughly 97%, you can still get a conventional loan but typically pay PMI until your equity grows; FHA loans go up to 96.5% LTV, and VA or USDA loans can reach 100% for eligible buyers. You lower your LTV by putting more down, paying down the balance, or benefiting from rising home value.

Frequently asked questions

What is loan-to-value (LTV)?
LTV is the loan amount divided by the property's appraised value or purchase price, expressed as a percentage. A $320,000 loan on a $400,000 home is an 80% LTV. Lower LTV means you have more equity and represent less risk to a lender, which can earn you better rates and terms.
Why does LTV affect PMI?
On conventional loans, an LTV above 80% (less than 20% down) usually requires private mortgage insurance (PMI), which protects the lender if you default. Once your LTV drops to about 80% through payments or appreciation, you can typically request PMI removal, and lenders must cancel it at 78% based on the original schedule.
What LTV do different loans allow?
Conventional loans can go up to about 97% LTV (3% down) with PMI. FHA loans allow up to 96.5% LTV (3.5% down). VA and USDA loans can reach 100% for eligible borrowers. The lower your LTV, the more loan programs and pricing you'll qualify for.
How do I lower my LTV?
Make a larger down payment, pay down the loan balance, or benefit from rising property value (which raises the denominator). A new appraisal showing a higher value can lower your LTV and may let you drop PMI sooner.
