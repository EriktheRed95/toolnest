How Much Rent Can I Afford?

Find out how much rent you can afford based on your income and the 30% rule. See comfortable, moderate, and stretch budgets, plus the income most landlords require.

How much rent can you really afford?

The classic answer is the 30% rule: keep rent at or below 30% of your gross (pre-tax) monthly income. It's a useful anchor, but real life is more nuanced — your debts, savings goals, and city all matter. This calculator shows a range instead of a single number: a comfortable budget (25%), the standard guideline (30%), and a stretch ceiling (35%), so you can choose where you want to sit.

What landlords are looking for

It's not just your budget — landlords have their own bar. Most require your gross monthly income to be at least 3 times the rent (some accept 2.5x, stricter ones want more). That income multiple often sets the practical ceiling on what you'll be approved for, regardless of what you'd be willing to pay. The calculator shows the maximum rent that clears the multiple you select, so you know what you can actually qualify for.

Don't forget your other obligations

The 30% rule looks only at rent, but your budget has to cover everything. If you carry meaningful debt payments — car loans, student loans, credit cards — those compete with rent for the same paycheck. Enter them above and the calculator shows a rent level that keeps your total obligations in a healthy range, which is often lower than 30% alone would suggest. And remember: gross income pays the landlord, but your take-home pay actually funds your life — budget against both.

Estimate only. Affordability depends on your full financial picture — taxes, savings, utilities, and lifestyle. Use this as a planning guide, not a guarantee of approval. This is not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much of my income should go to rent?
A common guideline is the 30% rule: spend no more than 30% of your gross (pre-tax) monthly income on rent. On a $5,000 monthly income, that's about $1,500. It's a starting point, not a hard limit — high earners can often spend less than 30% comfortably, while those in expensive cities sometimes stretch higher.
What income do landlords require to rent?
Many landlords require that your gross monthly income be at least 3 times the monthly rent (some use 2.5x or 40x the monthly rent as annual income). So for $1,500 rent, they'd want roughly $4,500/month in income. This calculator shows the maximum rent that meets the income multiple you choose.
Should rent be based on gross or net income?
The 30% rule traditionally uses gross income (before taxes), which is also what landlords verify. But for your own budgeting, it's wise to also check rent against your take-home pay — especially if you have high taxes, debt payments, or savings goals, since those come out of net income.
What if I have a lot of debt?
Existing debt payments — car loans, student loans, credit cards — eat into what you can realistically afford, even if the 30% rule says otherwise. If your debts are significant, lean toward the lower (comfortable) end of the range so your total obligations stay manageable.
