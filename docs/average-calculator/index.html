Find the mean, median, and mode

Paste a list of numbers — separated by commas, spaces, or new lines — and the calculator instantly works out the three most common measures of center. The mean is the everyday average. The median is the middle value, which resists outliers. The mode is the most frequent value, and the tool flags when several tie or when there's no mode at all. Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between mean, median, and mode?
The mean is the sum of all values divided by how many there are. The median is the middle value when the numbers are sorted. The mode is the value that appears most often. Each describes the 'center' of your data in a different way.
When should I use the median instead of the mean?
Use the median when your data has outliers or is skewed, such as incomes or house prices. A few very large or very small values pull the mean away from the typical value, but the median stays in the middle and is more representative.
What if there's no single mode?
If every value appears the same number of times, there's no mode. If two or more values tie for the most frequent, the data is multimodal and the calculator lists all of them. This is normal and just means several values are equally common.
