Discount & Sale Price Calculator

Calculate the sale price and how much you save with a percent-off discount. Stack a second discount or add sales tax to find your real final price.

What you'll actually pay

A "25% off" tag is easy; the real total gets murkier once a second markdown or sales tax enters the picture. This calculator gives you the bottom line — the sale price, your total savings, and the out-the-door price after tax. To find a sale price by hand, multiply the original by one minus the discount: a 25% discount means you pay 75%, so $80 × 0.75 = $60.

The stacked-discount trap

"Take an extra 10% off sale prices!" sounds like 30% when the rack already says 20% off — but it isn't. Extra discounts apply to the already-reduced price, so they multiply rather than add: 20% then 10% is 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, a 28% total reduction. The more discounts stack, the bigger the gap between the advertised sum and the real savings. This tool applies each discount in sequence so you see the true effective percentage.

Sales tax then lands on the final discounted amount, since that's what you actually pay. Add your local rate above for the genuine total.

Store policies vary — some coupons apply to the original price, and a few items are excluded from stacking. Use this for quick comparisons, and check the fine print at checkout.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percent-off discount?
Multiply the original price by the discount percentage to get the savings, then subtract it. A 25% discount on $80 saves $80 × 0.25 = $20, for a $60 sale price. You can also multiply the price by (1 − discount): $80 × 0.75 = $60.
How do stacked discounts work?
Additional discounts usually apply to the already-reduced price, not the original — so they don't simply add up. A 20% discount followed by an extra 10% off is 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, a 28% total reduction, not 30%. This calculator applies a second discount to the post-first-discount price.
Is sales tax added before or after the discount?
Tax is normally calculated on the final discounted price — the amount you actually pay — not the original price. Enter a tax rate above to see the true out-the-door total after discounts.
How do I find the original price from a sale price?
Divide the sale price by (1 − the discount as a decimal). If an item is $60 after 25% off, the original was 60 ÷ 0.75 = $80. This is handy for checking whether a 'sale' is as deep as advertised.
