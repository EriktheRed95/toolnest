Unit Converter

Convert between units of length, weight, temperature, and volume. Pick a category, enter a value, and choose your 'from' and 'to' units for an instant result.

One tool for everyday conversions

From following a recipe to reading a foreign spec sheet, everyday life is full of moments where you need to switch between units. This converter handles the four categories that come up most: length, weight, temperature, and volume. Pick a category, type a value, and choose what you're converting from and to — the answer appears instantly and updates as you type.

Most conversions work by routing through a base unit. A length is turned into meters using an exact factor, then into your target unit; weight uses grams and volume uses liters the same way. That keeps results consistent no matter which pair of units you pick.

Why temperature is different

Temperature scales don't share a common zero, so you can't convert them with a simple multiplier. Instead, this tool converts your value to Celsius first, then out to the unit you want, using the standard formulas. Note that volume measures here are US customary, so a "gallon" or "cup" may differ from UK imperial equivalents.

Conversion factors are standard definitions; results are estimates only where rounding is shown.

Frequently asked questions

What units can I convert?
This tool covers four common categories: length (meters, kilometers, miles, feet, inches and more), weight (grams, kilograms, pounds, ounces, tonnes), temperature (Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin), and volume (liters, milliliters, US gallons, quarts, cups, fluid ounces).
How does the conversion work?
Each category has a base unit. Length and weight values are converted to the base unit using a fixed factor, then to the target unit. Temperature uses formulas instead of factors because the scales don't share a zero point.
Are US or imperial gallons used?
Volume conversions use US customary measures — US gallons, quarts, pints, cups, and fluid ounces. These differ from UK imperial measures, so double-check if you're working with British recipes or fuel figures.
How accurate are the results?
Conversion factors are exact or standard definitions (for example, one inch is exactly 0.0254 meters), so results are accurate to the precision shown. Rounding only happens in the final display.
