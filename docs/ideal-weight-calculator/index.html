Ideal Weight Calculator

Estimate your ideal body weight from your height and sex using the Devine, Robinson, and Miller formulas, plus the healthy weight range from BMI.

What "ideal weight" really means

Ideal body weight formulas estimate a reference weight from your height and sex. The best-known — Devine, Robinson, and Miller — were developed decades ago, originally to help calculate medication doses, and each returns a slightly different figure. Showing all three together makes it obvious that there is no single magic number; rather, there is a reasonable band of healthy weights.

That's why this calculator also displays the healthy weight range from body mass index, the span of weights that puts your BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 for your height. A range is usually more useful than a single target because it acknowledges natural variation in build and body composition. None of these methods account for muscle mass, frame size, or age.

  • Devine — the most commonly cited formula in clinical settings.
  • Robinson — tends to produce slightly lower estimates.
  • Miller — tends to produce slightly higher estimates.

Frequently asked questions

What is ideal body weight?
Ideal body weight (IBW) is a reference figure estimated from your height and sex. Several classic formulas — Devine, Robinson, and Miller — each give a slightly different number. They were originally created for medication dosing, so treat them as rough guides rather than personal targets.
Which formula should I use?
There's no single correct one. Devine is the most widely cited, Robinson tends to give slightly lower figures, and Miller higher. Because they differ, this tool shows all three side by side along with the healthy weight range from BMI, which accounts for a span rather than a single point.
Why show a BMI range too?
The classic formulas return one number per height, but healthy weight is really a range. The BMI healthy band (18.5–24.9) for your height gives a realistic span of weights, which is usually more useful than any single 'ideal' figure.
Are these formulas accurate?
They ignore body composition, frame size, age, and muscle mass, so they're approximations. A muscular person may sit well above their 'ideal' weight while being perfectly healthy. Use these numbers as a ballpark and consult a professional for personal goals.
