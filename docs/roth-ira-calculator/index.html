Roth IRA Calculator

Estimate how much your Roth IRA could grow tax-free. Enter your balance, annual contribution, time horizon, and expected return to project your tax-free retirement savings.

The power of tax-free growth

A Roth IRA is one of the most valuable retirement accounts available, and its advantage is simple: you contribute money you've already paid tax on, and in return every dollar of qualified growth comes out tax-free in retirement. This calculator projects your balance by adding your annual contribution and compounding the total once a year at your expected return, then separates what you put in from what the account earned.

Because the growth is never taxed, the longer your money stays invested the more the Roth pulls ahead of a comparable taxable account. Over a 30- or 40-year horizon, sheltering decades of compounding from tax can mean keeping far more of your final balance.

  • Contribute early each year — funding in January rather than December gives the money extra months to compound.
  • Max it out when you can — the annual limit is use-it-or-lose-it; you can't make up missed years later.
  • Mind the income limits — direct contributions phase out at higher incomes.

Estimate only. This assumes a steady annual return and constant contributions; real markets fluctuate and returns aren't guaranteed. Contribution and income limits change yearly — verify current IRS figures. This is not financial or tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Roth IRA?
A Roth IRA is a retirement account you fund with after-tax dollars. Because you've already paid tax on the money going in, qualified withdrawals in retirement — including all the investment growth — come out completely tax-free. That tax-free growth is the Roth's biggest advantage over a traditional IRA, where contributions may be deductible but withdrawals are taxed.
What are the 2025 Roth IRA contribution limits?
For 2025 you can contribute up to $7,000 across all your IRAs, or $8,000 if you're age 50 or older thanks to the catch-up provision. These are annual limits, not per account. The default in this calculator uses $7,000 — adjust it down if you plan to contribute less.
Are there income limits for a Roth IRA?
Yes. The ability to contribute directly phases out at higher incomes — for 2025 the phase-out begins around $146,000 for single filers and $230,000 for married couples filing jointly, with contributions disallowed above the top of each range. Higher earners sometimes use a 'backdoor' Roth conversion instead. Check current IRS figures for your situation.
Why is Roth growth so valuable?
In a taxable account, dividends and gains can be taxed along the way and at withdrawal. In a Roth, decades of compounding happen with no tax drag and no tax due when you take qualified distributions. Over a long horizon, sheltering that growth from tax can mean keeping tens of thousands of dollars more.
