Down Payment Savings Calculator

Find out how long it will take to save your target home down payment based on your current savings, monthly contributions, and interest earned.

Planning your down payment timeline

Saving for a home is mostly about consistency and time. This calculator takes your target down payment — the home price times your chosen percentage — and figures out how long it takes to get there from your current savings plus a steady monthly contribution. It compounds the balance each month at your savings APY, so the interest you earn shortens the wait.

A 20% down payment is the classic benchmark because it avoids private mortgage insurance, but plenty of loans accept far less — so set the percentage to match your plan. Keeping your savings in a high-yield account rather than a checking account can meaningfully speed things up, especially on longer timelines. Try a few different monthly amounts to see how even modest increases pull your target date closer.

  • Remember closing costs — they add roughly 2%–5% of the loan on top of the down payment.
  • Automate it — a recurring transfer makes the monthly number realistic.

Estimate only. Actual savings growth depends on rate changes, taxes on interest, and how steadily you contribute. This is not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I save for a down payment?
A 20% down payment lets you avoid private mortgage insurance and often earns a better rate, but it isn't required. Many loans allow far less — 3.5% for FHA or even 0% for VA and USDA. Set your target percentage above to match the loan and home price you have in mind.
Does interest on my savings really matter?
It helps, especially over several years. A high-yield savings account or money market at around 4% APY grows your balance faster than a checking account at near 0%. The longer your timeline and the larger your balance, the more the compounding interest shortens the time to reach your goal.
Should I include closing costs in my goal?
This calculator targets the down payment only. Closing costs typically add another 2%–5% of the loan amount on top. If you want to save for both, raise your target percentage or add a separate savings goal so you're not caught short at the closing table.
What if I can't contribute every month?
Saving is rarely perfectly steady. Use a realistic average monthly contribution rather than your best month. You can rerun the numbers with a higher or lower amount to see how even small changes to your monthly savings move your target date.
