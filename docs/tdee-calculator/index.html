TDEE & Calorie Calculator

Estimate your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) and BMR using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, plus daily calorie targets for losing, maintaining, or gaining weight.

For adults 18+ only. Not for pregnancy, breastfeeding, children or teens. Activity factors are rough assumptions; these are not prescribed calorie targets.

How many calories you really need

Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is the number of calories you burn across a full day — resting metabolism plus everything you do, from walking to the gym to fidgeting at your desk. Knowing it is the foundation of any weight goal: eat at your TDEE to stay steady, below it to lose, above it to gain. This calculator starts from your Basal Metabolic Rate using the well-regarded Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then scales it by how active you are.

Choose the activity level that honestly matches your week. People tend to overestimate, so if you're unsure, pick the lower option — it's easier to add calories later than to wonder why the scale isn't moving.

Setting a calorie target

The targets translate your TDEE into practical daily numbers. Adding or subtracting 250 or 500 calories is shown only as arithmetic. It does not predict a fixed weekly weight change: metabolism and energy needs change with body weight. Values under 1,000 kcal/day are not displayed; higher values are not automatically appropriate or safe for you.

Estimates only. These figures are a starting point, not medical or dietary advice. Speak to a doctor or dietitian for guidance tailored to you.

References: Mifflin–St Jeor resting-energy equation and NIDDK Body Weight Planner scope (reviewed September 20, 2026).

Frequently asked questions

What is TDEE?
TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total number of calories you burn in a day, including your resting metabolism plus all movement and activity. Eating at your TDEE keeps your weight stable; eating below it leads to loss and above it to gain.
What is BMR?
BMR is your Basal Metabolic Rate, the calories your body burns at complete rest just to stay alive. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, one of the most accurate BMR formulas, then multiplies it by an activity factor to get your TDEE.
How do I lose or gain weight?
The displayed adjustments are arithmetic examples, not personalized intake targets or predictions of weight change. Energy needs change over time; discuss an appropriate plan with a qualified professional.
How accurate are these numbers?
They're well-researched estimates, but individual metabolism varies. Use them as a starting point, track your weight over a couple of weeks, and adjust your intake based on what actually happens.
