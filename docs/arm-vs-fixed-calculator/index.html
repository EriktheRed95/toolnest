ARM vs. Fixed Mortgage Calculator

Compare an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) against a fixed-rate loan. See the intro-period savings, the payment after the rate adjusts, total cost, and the break-even point.

ARM or fixed — which mortgage wins?

A fixed-rate mortgage locks your rate and payment for the entire loan: total predictability. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) offers a lower rate for an introductory period — the "5" in a 5/1 ARM means five years — then adjusts with the market. The trade is simple to state and hard to judge: the ARM saves you money now in exchange for uncertainty later. This calculator quantifies both sides over the time you actually expect to keep the loan.

It comes down to how long you'll stay

The single biggest factor is your time horizon:

  • If you'll sell, refinance, or pay off before the intro period ends, the ARM is often the clear winner — you bank the lower intro rate and leave before the rate ever adjusts.
  • If you'll stay well past the intro period, the fixed rate's certainty usually wins, because the ARM payment can jump once it starts adjusting, and you can't count on being able to refinance away from it.

Set your holding period and an honest estimate for the post-adjustment rate. ARMs have caps that limit increases, but the prudent move is to stress-test the worst case (the start rate plus the lifetime cap) and make sure you could still afford the payment if rates rise.

Estimate only. This model uses a single representative rate after the intro period; real ARMs adjust on a schedule against an index plus margin, within caps. It also doesn't include taxes, insurance, or refinance costs. Read the loan's terms and confirm caps and index with your lender. This is not financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM)?
An ARM has a fixed introductory rate for an initial period — like the '5' in a 5/1 ARM means 5 years — after which the rate adjusts periodically based on a market index plus a margin. The intro rate is usually lower than a comparable fixed rate, trading early savings for uncertainty later.
When does an ARM make sense?
An ARM can pay off if you expect to sell, refinance, or pay off the loan before or shortly after the intro period ends, since you capture the lower intro rate and leave before the risk arrives. If you plan to stay long-term, a fixed rate removes the uncertainty of future adjustments.
How high can an ARM payment go?
ARMs have caps that limit how much the rate can rise at the first adjustment, at each later adjustment, and over the life of the loan. The worst case is the start rate plus the lifetime cap. Enter a realistic — or worst-case — adjusted rate to stress-test what your payment could become.
What does 5/1 or 7/6 mean on an ARM?
The first number is the years the rate stays fixed; the second is how often it adjusts afterward. A 5/1 ARM is fixed for 5 years, then adjusts once a year. A 7/6 ARM is fixed for 7 years, then adjusts every 6 months. This calculator models the fixed intro period and a single representative rate afterward.
