Understanding your mortgage payment

Your monthly housing cost is usually more than just the loan. Lenders bundle it into PITI — Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance — and collect the tax and insurance portions in an escrow account so the big annual bills are spread across 12 months. If your down payment is under 20%, most conventional loans add private mortgage insurance (PMI), and homes in a community association add HOA dues. This calculator adds all of these so the number you see is closer to what you'll actually pay.

The principal-and-interest portion is fixed for the life of a fixed-rate loan, but in the early years most of each payment goes to interest. The amortization schedule above shows how the balance falls and how the principal/interest split shifts over time — which is why making extra principal payments early saves the most interest.

Tip for buyers: taxes and insurance vary a lot by area. Property tax is often quoted as a percentage of value (roughly 0.5%–2.5%/yr depending on the state and county), and insurance depends on the home and location. Plug in local figures for a realistic estimate, then confirm everything with a lender's official Loan Estimate before you commit.
