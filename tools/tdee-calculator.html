How many calories you really need

Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is the number of calories you burn across a full day — resting metabolism plus everything you do, from walking to the gym to fidgeting at your desk. Knowing it is the foundation of any weight goal: eat at your TDEE to stay steady, below it to lose, above it to gain. This calculator starts from your Basal Metabolic Rate using the well-regarded Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then scales it by how active you are.

Choose the activity level that honestly matches your week. People tend to overestimate, so if you're unsure, pick the lower option — it's easier to add calories later than to wonder why the scale isn't moving.

Setting a calorie target

The targets translate your TDEE into practical daily numbers. A roughly 500-calorie daily deficit tends to produce about half a kilogram of loss per week, while 250 gives a gentler pace that's easier to sustain; surpluses work the same way for gaining. Pick a target, eat consistently, and weigh yourself over two or three weeks before judging whether to adjust.
