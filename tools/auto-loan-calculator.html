What goes into your car payment

A car's sticker price is only the start. Your actual monthly payment depends on the amount financed — the price plus sales tax and fees, minus your down payment and any trade-in — together with your interest rate and loan length. This calculator assembles all of it so you can see the real payment and, just as important, the total interest over the life of the loan.

In most states, the trade-in lowers the taxable amount, so trading in a car can save you sales tax on top of reducing the loan. This tool taxes the price after the trade-in, which matches the common rule; if your state taxes the full price, set the trade-in to zero and account for it separately.

The trap of long car loans

Stretching to a 72- or 84-month loan is tempting because the monthly number looks friendly — but it's an expensive habit. Longer terms pile on interest and keep you underwater (owing more than the car is worth) for years, since cars depreciate faster than long loans pay down. A larger down payment and the shortest term you can comfortably afford are the two levers that save you the most.

Before you accept dealer financing, get pre-approved by a bank or credit union. Walking in with your own rate gives you a benchmark and real negotiating power — sometimes the dealer beats it, sometimes they don't.
