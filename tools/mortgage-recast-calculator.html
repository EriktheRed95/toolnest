Lower your payment without refinancing

A mortgage recast is one of the most underused tools in home finance. You make a large lump-sum payment toward your principal, and your lender re-amortizes the loan — recalculating your monthly payment over the same remaining term at the same interest rate. Because the balance is smaller, the payment drops. There's no refinance, no new rate, no credit check, and usually just a small flat fee.

That makes a recast especially appealing when you already have a low rate you don't want to lose. Refinancing into today's higher rates to lower a payment can backfire; recasting keeps your existing rate and simply puts your extra cash to work.

Recast vs. refinance vs. paying extra

  • Recast — keeps your rate and payoff date, lowers the required monthly payment. Best when cash flow flexibility is the goal and you have a lump sum (an inheritance, bonus, or home-sale proceeds).
  • Refinance — replaces the loan entirely. Worth it mainly when you can secure a meaningfully lower rate or need to change the term, and only if the savings beat the closing costs.
  • Paying extra (no recast) — applies the lump sum but keeps your current payment, so the loan is paid off early. This saves the same interest as a recast while shortening the term — the fastest path to debt-free, if you don't need the lower payment.

Estimate only. Not all loans are eligible to recast (many government-backed loans aren't), minimum lump sums and fees vary by servicer, and figures here use principal and interest only. Confirm the details with your loan servicer. This is not financial advice.
