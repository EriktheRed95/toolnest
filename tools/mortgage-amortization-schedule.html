How amortization works

An amortizing loan is repaid in equal monthly payments, but the split between interest and principal shifts over time. Each month, interest is charged on the remaining balance first; whatever is left of your payment reduces the principal. Because the balance is largest at the start, your earliest payments are mostly interest. As the balance shrinks, more of every payment goes to principal — which is why progress feels slow in the early years and accelerates later.

This is also why extra principal payments are so powerful early in the loan. Every extra dollar permanently removes future interest on that dollar for the rest of the term.
