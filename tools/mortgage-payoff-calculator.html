How extra payments crush mortgage interest

A mortgage front-loads interest: in the early years, most of each payment covers interest, not principal. That's why extra principal payments are so powerful — every additional dollar permanently removes the interest that dollar would have generated for the rest of the loan. The effect compounds, and starting early multiplies it.

This calculator compares your loan on its original schedule against the same loan with extra payments — a recurring monthly amount, a one-time lump sum, or both — and shows two numbers that tend to surprise people: how many years earlier you'd be debt-free, and how many thousands in interest you'd never pay.

Make sure it actually goes to principal

The catch is mechanical: extra money only accelerates payoff if your servicer applies it to principal. Some lenders instead credit it toward your next payment or park it as "unapplied funds." When you send extra, specify "apply to principal" — most have a field for it — and confirm it on your next statement.

Pay down or invest?

Extra payments earn a guaranteed, risk-free return equal to your interest rate. With a high rate, that's hard to beat. If your rate is low, investing the same money might earn more over time — at the cost of market risk and the certainty of being debt-free. There's no universally right answer; this tool quantifies the payoff side so you can weigh it against your alternatives.
