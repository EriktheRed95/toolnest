How much can you borrow against your home?

Your home equity — the difference between what your home is worth and what you owe — is one of the largest assets most people have. A HELOC (home equity line of credit) or a home equity loan lets you turn part of that equity into cash. But you can't borrow against all of it: lenders limit your combined loan-to-value (CLTV), usually to 80%–90% of the home's value.

The math is straightforward. Take the home's value times the CLTV cap, then subtract your current mortgage balance — what's left is your available credit. This calculator runs that for you, and also estimates the monthly payment on an amount you'd borrow, both as interest-only (typical during a HELOC's draw period) and fully amortizing (during repayment).

HELOC vs. home equity loan

  • HELOC — a revolving line you draw from as needed, usually at a variable rate. Many have a draw period with interest-only payments, followed by a repayment period. Flexible, but payments can rise with rates.
  • Home equity loan — a one-time lump sum at a fixed rate, repaid in level installments. Predictable, best when you know exactly how much you need.

Both are secured by your home, which is what makes their rates lower than credit cards — and also what makes them riskier. If you can't repay, the lender can foreclose. That's why equity borrowing is best reserved for value-adding home improvements or consolidating costlier debt, not everyday spending.

Estimate only. Actual limits depend on your credit, income, the lender's CLTV cap, and a current appraisal of your home. Variable HELOC rates change over time. Confirm terms with lenders before borrowing. This is not financial advice.
