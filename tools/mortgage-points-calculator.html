Should you buy down your mortgage rate?

Discount points let you pay extra at closing in exchange for a lower interest rate for the life of the loan. One point costs 1% of the loan amount and usually trims the rate by about a quarter percent. It's a trade of upfront cash for lower monthly payments — and whether it's smart comes down to one number: the break-even point.

The break-even is how long it takes your monthly savings to repay what the points cost. Buy $4,000 in points to save $80 a month, and you recover the cost in 50 months. Stay in the loan longer than that and the points are pure savings; leave sooner — by selling or refinancing — and you've lost money.

When points make sense (and when they don't)

  • Worth it: you'll keep this loan for many years, you're confident you won't refinance soon, and you have cash to spare at closing without shrinking your down payment.
  • Skip them: you might move or refinance within a few years, or that cash would do more as a larger down payment (avoiding PMI) or in reserves.
