How a 1031 exchange defers tax

When you sell an appreciated investment property outright, you owe tax on the realized gain — the sale price minus selling costs and your adjusted cost basis. That gain is taxed in two layers: depreciation you previously deducted is "recaptured" (often at up to 25%), and the remaining appreciation is taxed at capital gains rates, plus any state income tax. A Section 1031 like-kind exchange lets you roll the entire proceeds into a replacement investment property and postpone all of that tax, carrying your old basis forward.

This calculator estimates the tax you would defer by splitting your gain into a depreciation-recapture portion and a capital-gains portion, applying your rates, and adding state tax. It also shows the net proceeds available to reinvest. Remember the deferral is conditional: you must use a qualified intermediary, reinvest all proceeds into equal-or-greater value, replace any paid-off debt, and hit the strict 45-day and 180-day deadlines.
