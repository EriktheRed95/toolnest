Three quick screens for a rental

Investors look at dozens of properties for every one they buy, so they need fast filters. This calculator runs three of the classics at once. The cap rate divides net operating income by price to estimate an all-cash yield, letting you compare properties regardless of how they're financed. The gross rent multiplier divides price by annual rent — a lower number means you pay less per dollar of rent. The 1% rule checks whether monthly rent reaches 1% of the purchase price, a rough threshold for whether a deal can cash flow.

None of these is a verdict. They're tripwires that tell you which listings deserve a real underwrite and which to skip. A property can pass the 1% rule and still lose money after a big roof repair, or fail it and shine because of appreciation potential.

  • Cap rate — NOI ÷ price. Ignores your loan; describes the property.
  • GRM — price ÷ gross rent. Ignores expenses; a blunt valuation check.
  • 1% rule — monthly rent ÷ price. A go/no-go shortlist filter.

Estimate only. These are screening metrics, not a full analysis. They omit financing, vacancy, capital expenditures, and market trends. Verify rents and expenses and underwrite carefully before buying. This is not financial or investment advice.
