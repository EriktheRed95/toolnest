Snowball or avalanche — get out of debt faster

When you're juggling several debts, the order you attack them in matters more than most people realize. Two strategies dominate, and they optimize for different things:

  • Debt avalanche — pay minimums on everything, then throw every extra dollar at the highest-interest debt first. This minimizes total interest and is almost always the fastest, cheapest path mathematically.
  • Debt snowball — pay minimums on everything, then attack the smallest balance first. You clear individual debts quickly, and those early wins build momentum that helps many people stick with the plan.

This calculator simulates both on your actual debts and shows the difference in months and interest, so you can weigh the avalanche's savings against the snowball's motivational payoff.

The real engine: extra payments

Minimum payments are structured to keep you in debt for years, especially on high-rate credit cards. The single biggest factor in how fast you get free isn't which method you pick — it's how much extra you can put toward the target debt each month. As each debt is cleared, roll its payment into the next one; that growing payment is what gives the snowball (and avalanche) their power.
