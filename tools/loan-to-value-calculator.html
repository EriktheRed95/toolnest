Understanding loan-to-value

Loan-to-value compares how much you owe to what the property is worth. It's one of the first numbers a lender looks at, because it measures their risk: a high LTV means you have little equity cushion, so a default is more likely to leave the lender short. That's why a lower LTV usually unlocks better interest rates, more loan options, and — on conventional loans — freedom from private mortgage insurance.

The 80% threshold is the one to remember. At or below 80% LTV (at least 20% equity), conventional borrowers generally skip PMI. Between 80% and roughly 97%, you can still get a conventional loan but typically pay PMI until your equity grows; FHA loans go up to 96.5% LTV, and VA or USDA loans can reach 100% for eligible buyers. You lower your LTV by putting more down, paying down the balance, or benefiting from rising home value.
