How the seller net sheet works

When you sell a home, the sale price is not what you pocket. From the price, the closing process subtracts what you still owe on your mortgage plus the costs of selling. What's left is your net proceeds — the wire or check you receive at closing. This calculator builds a simplified version of the "net sheet" your agent or escrow officer prepares.

The biggest line items are usually:

  • Mortgage payoff — your remaining loan balance, plus any prepayment interest.
  • Real estate commission — historically 5%–6% of the price, increasingly negotiable. Enter the total rate for both sides as your contract specifies.
  • Title & escrow / settlement fees — for handling the closing, title search, and title insurance, per local custom.
  • Transfer / excise tax — a government fee on the sale that varies widely by state and county (low in Oklahoma; often paid by the seller in much of California).
  • Concessions, repairs, and credits — anything you agreed to credit the buyer.

Estimate only. Actual costs depend on your state, county, contract, loan, and the closing date (which affects tax and interest prorations). This tool is for planning and is not legal, tax, or financial advice — your agent, title/escrow officer, and a tax professional can give exact figures for your sale.
