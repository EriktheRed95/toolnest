Adding and removing sales tax

Sales tax is a percentage added to a purchase at checkout. To add it, multiply the price by the tax rate and add it back on — an 8% rate turns a $50 item into $54. This calculator handles that instantly, and it also runs the calculation in reverse: if you only know the tax-included total (say, from a receipt), tick the box above and it backs out the original price and the tax portion.

The reverse math trips people up because you can't just subtract 8%. Instead you divide the total by 1.08, since the total represents 108% of the original price. A $54 total at 8% comes from a $50 item, not $49.68.

Why rates vary so much

In the U.S., there's no single national sales tax. States set a base rate, and counties and cities often add their own, so the combined rate at a given address can range from zero (a few states have no sales tax) to over 10%. On top of that, categories like groceries, prescriptions, and clothing are sometimes taxed at lower rates or exempt entirely. For an accurate result, enter the combined local rate that applies to what you're buying.

This tool is for quick estimates. For exact tax on a specific purchase, rely on the merchant's checkout or your local tax authority's published rates.
