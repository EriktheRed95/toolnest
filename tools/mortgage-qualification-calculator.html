How lenders decide how much income you need

Mortgage qualification centers on your debt-to-income ratio (DTI) — the share of your gross monthly income that goes to debt payments. Lenders add your full housing payment (PITI: principal, interest, taxes, and insurance) to your other recurring debts like car loans, student loans, and minimum credit card payments, then check that total against a maximum percentage of your income. Many conventional loans cap the back-end DTI near 43%, so the income you need is simply your total obligations divided by that cap.

This calculator runs that math in reverse: it builds the PITI from your home price, down payment, rate, taxes, and insurance, adds your other debts, and divides by the DTI ceiling to show the annual income required. The front-end ratio shows housing alone as a share of that income. Lowering the price, increasing the down payment, paying down other debts, or securing a lower rate all reduce the income you need to qualify.
