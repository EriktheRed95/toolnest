How your GPA is calculated

Your grade point average condenses every course grade into a single number on a 4.0 scale. The process is a weighted average: each letter grade converts to grade points (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, and so on), which are multiplied by the course's credit hours to get quality points. Add up all the quality points, divide by the total credit hours, and you have your GPA.

This calculator handles the weighting automatically. Pick each course's grade, enter its credits, and add as many courses as you need — your GPA updates instantly. To project a future GPA, just add the courses you're taking with the grades you're aiming for.

Why credit hours change everything

Because GPA is weighted by credits, not all grades carry equal influence. An A in a 4-credit lab boosts your average more than an A in a 1-credit seminar — and a stumble in a heavy course costs you more. Understanding this helps you prioritize: when time is tight, the credit-heavy courses are where grades move your GPA the most.

Grade scales vary by school — some use plus/minus values, some don't, and weighted high-school GPAs add bonus points for honors or AP courses. This tool uses the common 4.0 scale shown above; check your institution's official policy for exact values.
