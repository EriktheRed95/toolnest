Does the rent cover the mortgage?

The debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) answers a single, decisive question for rental investors: does the property's income cover its debt payment? It's the ratio of income to the mortgage, and it's the heart of DSCR loans — a popular financing route that qualifies the property rather than your personal income.

For these loans, lenders typically compare the gross monthly rent against the full monthly payment, known as PITIA — Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance, and Association (HOA) dues. A DSCR of 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the payment; above 1.0 means positive coverage with a cushion; below 1.0 means the rent falls short.

What DSCR lenders want to see

  • 1.25 or higher — strong coverage; you'll generally get the best rates and terms.
  • 1.0 to 1.25 — acceptable to most DSCR lenders; the deal cash-flows but with a thinner margin.
  • Below 1.0 — the rent doesn't fully cover the payment. Some lenders still finance these (often at higher rates), but you'll usually need a bigger down payment to bring the ratio up.

If your ratio is low, the most direct fixes are a larger down payment (which lowers the principal-and-interest portion) or a property with stronger rent relative to price. Use this calculator to test how much down payment or rent it takes to clear your target lender's threshold before you apply.

Estimate only. Lenders define DSCR in slightly different ways — some use market rent from an appraisal, some net out vacancy or expenses. Confirm your lender's exact formula and minimum. This is not financial or lending advice.
