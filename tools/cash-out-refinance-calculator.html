What a cash-out refinance does

A cash-out refinance replaces your current mortgage with a larger new loan and hands you the difference in cash. The new balance is your existing balance plus the cash you take plus closing costs (if you roll them in). Because you're borrowing more against the same home, your loan-to-value rises — and lenders usually cap it around 80% of the appraised value on a primary residence.

This calculator shows the new loan amount, the monthly principal-and-interest payment at your new rate and term, the resulting LTV, and the maximum cash you could pull while staying under the cap. People use cash-out refinances to consolidate higher-rate debt, fund renovations, or cover large expenses — but it converts home equity into debt and often resets your payoff clock.

  • Watch the LTV cap — exceeding it can mean a denial or a worse rate.
  • Compare total interest, not just the monthly payment, before extending your term.
