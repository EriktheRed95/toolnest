Salary and hourly pay, both directions

Salaried and hourly figures describe the same income from different angles, and switching between them is one of the most useful things you can do when comparing job offers or planning a budget. This tool works both ways: the top section turns an annual salary into an hourly rate, and the bottom section turns an hourly rate into a yearly salary. Each has its own result box, so you can use one or compare the two side by side.

The key inputs are your hours per week and weeks per year. Standard full-time work is often modelled as 40 hours across 52 weeks — about 2,080 paid hours — but you can adjust both to match part-time schedules, unpaid leave, or overtime so the numbers reflect your real situation.

Reading the breakdown

Beyond the headline figure, each section breaks pay down into the periods you actually get paid in: hourly, daily, weekly, biweekly, monthly, and annual. That makes it easy to sanity-check a quoted rate against a paycheck or to see what a raise looks like per hour. Everything recalculates the moment you change a field.

All figures are gross estimates before taxes, benefits, and other deductions, which vary by location and circumstance.
