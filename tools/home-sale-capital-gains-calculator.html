Will you owe tax when you sell your home?

Many homeowners are relieved to learn that a big chunk — often all — of the profit from selling a primary residence is tax-free. The IRS lets qualifying sellers exclude up to $250,000 of gain (single) or $500,000 (married filing jointly), provided the home was your main residence for at least 2 of the last 5 years. Only gain above the exclusion is taxed.

The math runs in three steps:

  • Amount realized = sale price minus selling costs (agent commission, closing fees).
  • Cost basis = original purchase price plus the cost of qualifying improvements.
  • Capital gain = amount realized minus cost basis. Apply your exclusion, and what's left is the taxable gain.

Improvements are your secret weapon

Every dollar of qualifying capital improvement — an addition, new roof, remodeled kitchen, upgraded systems — raises your cost basis and lowers your taxable gain dollar-for-dollar. Routine repairs don't count, but real improvements do, so keep receipts for the entire time you own the home. For long-held homes in hot markets, documented improvements can be the difference between owing tax and owing nothing.

Estimate only. This is a simplified model. Actual tax depends on your full income (which sets your 0%/15%/20% rate), state taxes, the 3.8% net investment income tax for higher earners, depreciation recapture if you ever rented the home, and partial-exclusion rules.
