Your personal balance sheet

Net worth is the clearest single snapshot of your financial health. It works just like a company's balance sheet: add up everything you own, subtract everything you owe, and the difference is what's truly yours. This calculator groups your assets — cash, investments, retirement accounts, home, vehicles, and anything else of value — and your liabilities, from your mortgage to credit card balances, then computes the bottom line.

The exact figure matters less than its direction over time. As you pay down debt and accumulate savings and investments, your net worth should trend upward. Recalculating every six or twelve months turns vague feelings about money into a concrete trendline you can act on.

  • Grow assets — automate saving and investing so balances build without ongoing effort.
  • Shrink liabilities — paying off high-rate debt boosts net worth dollar for dollar.
  • Track the trend — compare snapshots over time rather than fixating on one number.

Estimate only. Asset values like homes and vehicles are estimates that change with the market, and this is a simplified snapshot, not a formal valuation. Use it to track your progress over time. This is not financial advice.
