What price per square foot tells you

Price per square foot is the real estate world's favorite shorthand. Divide a home's price by its finished square footage and you get a single number that lets you compare a 1,500 sq ft cottage against a 3,000 sq ft colonial on a roughly common footing. Buyers use it to sanity-check a list price, sellers use it to position against recent sales, and appraisers lean on it as one input among several.

The second section flips the math: pick a price per square foot from comparable sales nearby and multiply by a home's size to ballpark its value. It's a fast way to translate "homes around here go for about $210 a foot" into a dollar figure before you dig deeper.

  • Smaller homes usually carry a higher price per square foot than larger ones nearby.
  • Use consistent square footage — finished, above-grade area — when you compare.
  • Treat the result as a starting point, not an appraisal.

Estimate only. Price per square foot varies widely with condition, updates, lot size, layout, and location — two identical-sized homes can be worth very different amounts. Use comparable sales and a professional opinion of value before making decisions. This is not financial or real estate advice.
