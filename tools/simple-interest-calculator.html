How simple interest works

Simple interest is the most straightforward way to calculate the cost of borrowing or the return on a deposit. It applies a fixed annual rate to the original principal only, using the formula I = P × r × t. Because the interest base never changes, the amount grows in a straight line: doubling the time doubles the interest, and doubling the principal doubles it too. That predictability makes simple interest easy to reason about for short-term loans, certain bonds, and informal lending between people.

The key thing to watch for is whether an account or loan actually uses simple or compound interest. Compound interest adds each period's interest back to the balance, so future interest is calculated on a larger amount. Over short periods the gap is small, but over many years compounding pulls far ahead. When comparing offers, confirm the method and the compounding frequency, not just the headline rate.
